1Identity in one glance

Verified + independent + open state sit above the name. The name is the page's H1; category, neighbourhood, distance and rating follow. A customer arriving from search should immediately recognise the card they clicked.

2Prices are real and specific

Every service has a name, starting price, duration and short inclusion note. “From” is used where length or hair changes the price. The request flow confirms the final price in writing — no surprise charges.

3Hours communicate availability, not booking

Today is highlighted; closed days are explicit. “Open now” is derived from hours, not hand-set. The page says clearly that a request is not yet a confirmed appointment.

4Travel area without exposing a home

The map shows an approximate radius around the neighbourhood, never an exact home pin. Neighbourhood names are listed, with a stated travel fee. The customer knows whether the provider will come to them before requesting.

5Reviews tied to transactions

Only signed-in customers can review; a “Booked via HOLYDO” label appears when a completed request exists. Ratings show count and distribution, not just a score. Providers can flag a review, not delete it.

6Sticky request rail

On desktop the request card remains visible while reading services, hours and reviews. The primary CTA opens a sheet with service, date/window and contact details. “Message” is secondary so requests remain structured.

7Approval is part of the page

The approval card shows what was checked and when, not just a badge. It links to a plain-language explanation. If a provider is suspended, this page returns 404 and requests stop immediately.

8States & SEO

  • Not approved / suspended → 404 with support link.
  • No reviews yet → “New on HOLYDO” with verified profile note.
  • No photos → generic category cover, never a stock face.
  • Structured data: LocalBusiness + Service + AggregateRating, canonical URL, share image.

03BMobile

Photo first, then identity, then a persistent request bar. The page is long by nature; a compact anchor row (Services · Hours · Reviews) lets people jump without a maze.

ATwo-step request, no account wall

Step 1 captures service, date, window and contact. Step 2 is a confirmation/review of the request. An account is offered after sending, not required before — reducing drop-off while still allowing the provider to respond by email.

BContact details unlock after request

Showing a phone number on a public profile invites spam and moves conversations off-platform. Instead, the provider receives the request and can choose to call, text, or use the contact details the customer supplied.

CMobile anchors

The anchor row jumps to Services, Hours, Area, Reviews and Photos. Sticky request bar appears after the first scroll and stays above the home indicator. The page does not use the bottom tab bar to keep the CTA uncluttered.

DWhat must be true before launch

Approved-only 404s, structured service/price data, timezone-safe hours, approximate map circle, verified-review rules, rate-limited requests, and a clear cancellation/response policy. These are product requirements, not polish.

Design note: the profile should feel authored by the provider. Her words, her photos, her prices — HOLYDO provides the frame and the verification, not a generic template voice.
