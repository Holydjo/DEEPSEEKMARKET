1Categories are the second search

Eight category tiles with live provider counts. On mobile this becomes a horizontally scrollable row plus “All categories”, so it never pushes the nearby results below two screens.

2Cards carry the decision data

Photo, name, verified state, category, neighbourhood, rating, distance, service setting, and “from” price. A customer should be able to shortlist without opening every profile. “Nearby / Top rated / New” re-sorts in place; “See all” goes to the full results page.

3The approval story is explicit

The trust band explains what is checked and shows a real decision record. The badge is never used as decoration. This is also where admin rejection/resubmission can be explained honestly.

4Location is visible, never silent

The header keeps the current location editable. The hero search shows it in the second field. Geolocation is only requested after an explicit “Use my location” action, with city/neighbourhood as a fallback.

5Provider invitation comes after proof

Supply is invited only after the page has demonstrated demand and trust. The small dashboard mock previews exactly what they get. No commission is stated plainly; this is a launch promise, not a hidden upsell.

6Place links are first-class

Neighbourhoods are browsable, indexable destinations that link to pre-filtered search. This supports local SEO and the mental model “what’s near me?” better than a generic city dropdown alone.

7Mobile structure

Header condenses to logo + location. Search stacks into two fields and a full-width button. Categories become horizontal tiles. Provider cards become a horizontal swipe deck. The bottom tab bar holds Explore, Search, Saved, Account.

8States to design

  • Returning visitor: last city restored, “Near you” personalised.
  • No geolocation: clear city picker; no misleading distances.
  • No nearby providers: nearby neighbourhoods + category links, not a dead end.
  • Loading: skeleton cards at the exact final dimensions.

AThumb zone

Search, location and the primary button sit in the top 40% only because search is the landing intent. Primary actions on later screens (booking, request) move to the bottom edge. Bottom navigation is always reachable.

BOne decision per band

Each band has a single job: search, browse, compare nearby, trust, or become a provider. No carousel that hides the only useful content; horizontal swipes always show a visible next card.

CCompression, not deletion

Everything from desktop survives on mobile. Categories become 2×3 with “More”, provider cards become a swipe deck with the same decision data, and the trust band becomes a compact card.

DAbove the fold on a 844 px device

Visible without scrolling: logo, location, headline, service + location fields, search button, and the start of categories. Real content begins immediately — no full-screen splash or cookie wall.

Open question: on mobile, should “Close to you” come before categories? I propose categories first on a first visit (orientation), then remember the last intent and lift nearby providers on return.
